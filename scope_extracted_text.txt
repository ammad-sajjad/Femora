1. Introduction 
Women's health remains significantly underserved in the digital healthcare space. Research 
by Epstein D. A. (2017) [1] found that while menstrual cycle tracking apps are widely used, they are 
most accurate only for women with very regular cycles, whereas the women who need these 
applications most are those with irregular cycles. A study examining 10 menstrual cycle tracker apps 
by Worsfold et al. (2021) [2] found that 67% of ovulation predictions were 2-9 days early, 
demonstrating fundamental inaccuracy in current prediction approaches.
Three major health conditions further demonstrate the need for Femora. First, PCOS affects 
approximately 9.2% of women globally [4], with South Asian women at disproportionately higher 
risk, yet no widely accessible AI-powered screening tool exists for this population. Second, breast 
cancer 
is the most commonly occurring cancer in women worldwide [21]. In 2022 alone, 2.3 million 
women were diagnosed with 670,000 deaths [16]. In Pakistan specifically, breast cancer peaks at 
ages 40–50, a decade earlier than in Western populations [17], making early awareness tools 
critically important. Third, Pregnancy complications such as pre-eclampsia and eclampsia accounts 
for approximately one-third of maternal deaths in Pakistani tertiary hospitals [18] conditions that 
benefit significantly from early mobile-based monitoring. No single existing platform addresses all 
three of these needs together.
Femora aims to address this gap by providing an AI-powered women's health platform 
integrating menstrual cycle prediction using adaptive personalization, PCOS risk detection, 
pregnancy care, and a breast cancer module combining CNN-based ultrasound image classification 
with a symptom-based risk assessment model, all in one accessible application requiring no 
hardware.

3. Problem Statement 
Existing women’s health applications mainly focus on basic menstrual tracking using 
generalized statistical averages. These applications fail to provide accurate predictions for women 
with irregular cycles [1] because they do not adapt to individual lifestyle factors such as stress, sleep, 
BMI, and symptom variations [3]. As a result, many users receive inaccurate ovulation and cycle 
predictions, reducing the reliability and usefulness of current menstrual cycle tracking systems.
PCOS is one of the most common hormonal disorders affecting women, particularly in South 
Asian populations. Despite its prevalence, diagnosis is often delayed because accessible early 
screening tools are limited. Current health applications do not analyze cycle irregularity, symptom 
history, or BMI to provide intelligent PCOS risk assessment.
Breast cancer awareness and early detection support remain limited in women’s health 
applications. Most menstrual tracking systems do not include breast health monitoring, symptom-
based risk assessment, or medical image analysis capabilities such as ultrasound classification. As a 
result, users must rely on separate systems for reproductive health and breast health management. 
This separation limits early awareness and prevents users from accessing multiple women’s health 
services in one platform.
Pregnancy care applications mainly provide static guidance and reminders but rarely include 
intelligent symptom monitoring or anomaly detection. Additionally, pregnancy monitoring systems

4. Problem Solution / Objectives of the Proposed System 
Femora proposes an AI-powered mobile application using machine learning trained on real 
peer-reviewed clinical datasets. Li et al. (2022) [11] (Journal of the American Medical Informatics 
Association) demonstrated that predictive models accounting for user adherence significantly 
outperform simple averaging. 
4.1 Objectives 

To develop an AI-powered menstrual cycle tracking system capable of 
predicting cycle length, period duration, ovulation window, and daily cycle 
phases using clinical and symptom-based data

To implement adaptive personalization that improves prediction accuracy 
over time based on individual user history and symptom patterns.

To develop a PCOS/PCOD risk assessment module using factors such as 
cycle irregularity, BMI, and user-reported symptoms to provide early risk 
awareness.

To implement a breast cancer awareness module that supports AI-based 
ultrasound image classification and symptom-based risk assessment for early 
awareness assistance.

To include a pregnancy care module that offers trimester-based guidance, 
symptom monitoring, reminders, and anomaly flagging for high-risk 
conditions.

To provide hormonal health insights by analyzing symptom patterns across 
different menstrual cycle phases.

To develop an AI healthcare companion capable of answering user health-
related queries and providing personalized wellness support.

To provide visual health reports and dashboards showing cycle trends, 
symptom history, and prediction analytics

To ensure secure handling, privacy, and ethical management of sensitive user 
health data.
5. Scope 
The scope of Femora includes the development of an AI-powered mobile healthcare 
application that provides menstrual cycle prediction, PCOS risk assessment, hormonal health 
insights, pregnancy care, breast cancer awareness and detection support, and an AI healthcare 
companion within a single platform. The system utilizes machine learning models trained on peer-
reviewed clinical datasets for cycle prediction, daily phase detection, breast cancer image 
classification, and symptom-based risk assessment to deliver personalized healthcare insights and

6. Modules 
6.1 User Onboarding and Health

Collect baseline health information including age, BMI, cycle regularity, and existing 
conditions such as PCOS.

Build personalized user health profiles to initialize AI prediction models and address 
the cold-start problem.

Secure account creation with email authentication and profile management.
6.2 Menstrual Cycle Tracking and Prediction

Allow users to log menstrual cycle details including Menstruation dates, 
cramps, and daily symptoms, addressing tracking needs identified by Epstein 
et al. (2017) [1].

General Model predicts next cycle length and period duration from day one. 
70% accuracy on day one, improving with logged cycles (Random Forest, 
trained on Fehring dataset [8]).

Personalized Model adapts to specific woman's cycle pattern after 3+ cycles, 
giving 85-90% accuracy, (LSTM, trained on individual user history).

Display menstrual phases and predictions through an interactive calendar 
interface.

Detect delayed or irregular cycles and provide lifestyle-based explanatory 
insights, addressing the documented gap in Oxford Open Digital Health 
(2025) [3].
6.3 Breast Cancer Awareness and Detection
The breast cancer module consists of three integrated components:

Component A CNN Image Classification: User uploads a hospital ultrasound 
image (PNG/JPG). A ResNet50 CNN model using transfer learning, fine-
tuned on combined BUSI dataset [24] and Breast-Lesions-USG dataset [26] 

Component B Symptom-Based Risk Assessment: For users without an 
ultrasound scan, a questionnaire-based XGBoost model trained on the 
Wisconsin dataset [25] computes a risk score based on age, family history, 
menstrual history, and self-reported symptoms.



7. System Limitations/Constraints 

Femora does not provide clinical diagnoses. All risk scores and image classifications are for 
awareness purposes only and must be followed up with qualified medical professionals.

Prediction accuracy on day one is approximately 70%, improving to 90% after 3+ cycles. As 
noted by Worsfold et al. (2021) [2], there are currently no universally agreed-upon criteria 
for evaluating period prediction app accuracy. Femora's metrics are based on held-out real 
patient test data.

PCOS and breast cancer risk scores from the symptom-based model are derived from self-
reported data only they do not incorporate blood tests, 3d-scans or clinical examination data.

Wearable device integration for real-time physiological metrics is outside the current project 
scope and is planned as a future enhancement.

The system relies on consistent and honest user input. Inaccurate or irregular logging will 
reduce prediction quality.
